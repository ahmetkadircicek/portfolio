# Chalko: project facts

The project page is `/projects/chalko`. The privacy and support pages are `/chalko/privacy` and `/chalko/support`.

Source material for the portfolio project page. Everything here is true of the app as of 24 October 2026.

## One line

Chalko is a Turkish pass-and-play party game for iPhone and iPad. One player draws a word on a real whiteboard or paper while their team guesses; the app only deals the words, runs the timer and keeps score.

## How it is played

- 2 to 4 fixed teams: Kırmızılar, Maviler, Yeşiller, Sarılar. A short draw animation picks the team that starts.
- Before the game: turn length (30, 45, 60, 90 or 120 s), number of rounds (3, 5, 8 or 10) and free passes per turn (0 to 5). The settings screen shows an estimated game length, for example "≈ 14 dk".
- **Bildi** (guessed) is +1. **Pas** (skip) is free until the turn's free passes run out, then each pass is −1. Scores can go negative. Answer buttons ignore taps for 0.8 s after each answer, so a double tap cannot skip a word.
- The word stays hidden until the drawer presses and holds the card; each new word peeks open for 1.5 s, so teammates cannot read it off the screen.
- In the last 10 seconds the timer turns red and pulses, and a tick and a tock sound alternate and get louder.
- When time runs out the app asks whether the last word was guessed in time. The turn summary lists every word, and a tap flips a mis-pressed Bildi or Pas.
- Teams with equal top scores share the win. "Rövanş" starts a rematch with the same settings and a new draw.
- Four difficulty levels: Çöp Adam (easy), Karalamacı (medium), Ressam (hard) and Dalí (impossible, abstract concepts only). On the level screen nothing is preselected and the start button stays disabled until a level is picked. A row under the levels shows the current settings and opens them.
- Home has a quick start (Karalamacı, 3 teams, 45 s, 3 rounds) next to the normal Play button. The how-to-play card opens automatically on first launch.
- 2,607 hand-curated Turkish words: 834 / 618 / 569 / 586 per level. Every level above Çöp Adam also mixes in 300 words from the level below, giving pools of 834 / 918 / 869 / 886.
- A word never repeats within a game, and words not seen in earlier games come first.

## Role, platform, dates, status

- Solo project: idea, product decisions, design and development by Ahmet Kadir Çiçek, built with AI coding assistants (Google Antigravity).
- iOS 17+, iPhone and iPad, portrait only. On iPad the content sits in a centered column up to 600 pt wide. Turkish only. Free, offline, no ads, no accounts, no in-app purchases.
- Bundle identifier `com.ahmetkadircicek.chalko`.
- Started 16 October 2026.
- Status: not released on the App Store yet. The MVP is feature-complete and internal TestFlight builds have been uploaded (build 13 on 24 October 2026; the app now carries build 14).

## Technology

- Swift and SwiftUI with the Observation framework (`@Observable`). One state machine drives every screen: setup → handoff → countdown → playing → summary → game over.
- The timer is derived from an end date polled every 200 ms, so pausing is exact. The game pauses itself when the app leaves the foreground.
- Word lists are bundled `.txt` files, one word per line, deduplicated at load with Turkish-locale lowercasing.
- `UserDefaults` holds the settings and a per-level history of seen words. No network calls, no personal data, a privacy manifest is included, and the App Privacy answer is "Data Not Collected".
- Sound: 24 original marimba-style sounds (12 roles, several with variants that rotate), generated by a Python script with the standard library only, each with a natural decay instead of a hard cut. Played with `AVAudioPlayer` on an ambient audio session (respects the silent switch and mixes with music already playing). Every button has a click sound and a haptic whose strength depends on how important the button is. Haptics use `UIFeedbackGenerator`. Separate sound and haptics toggles.
- Home has a faint rain of the game's own words falling in the background; every few seconds a green hand-drawn ring circles one word. It is drawn with `TimelineView` and `Canvas`, and shows a single still frame when Reduce Motion is on.
- StoreKit review request after the third finished game.
- Project generated with XcodeGen.
- Testing: no unit tests. A UI test target has five UI tests (a full-flow checklist, rematch and game-over flows, the answer cooldown and a screenshot capture). The game was play-tested in the Simulator on iPhone SE, iPhone 17 Pro Max and iPad Pro 13", and a shell script checks the word lists for duplicates and cross-level overlaps.

## Design decisions

- A chalkboard look, drawn first in a design canvas and then ported to SwiftUI: cream paper (#FFF3DC), a deep green board (#2E6F5E), warm ink (#2F2A24), chunky outlines with hard drop shadows, and Baloo 2 (bundled, SIL OFL) for all text.
- The Chalko mark is a green chalkboard in a chalk-colored frame (#F2C98B) with a tray and two pieces of chalk. The app icon is the green board with the white wordmark and the tray; the launch screen is the same green with the white wordmark.
- The whole background takes the active team's pastel: red #FFC9D1, blue #BFE0FF, green #C3EBD6, yellow #FFEDB0. Small markers use stronger dots (#F0647A, #5BA8F0, #3DB383, #F5C242). Danger red is #C9372F.
- Each level has its own tint and icon on the level screen: Çöp Adam, Karalamacı, Ressam and Dalí.
- The app was first called Tahtaya Kalk; it was renamed to Chalko on 22 October 2026, and the look moved from a blue, yellow and lime cartoon style to the chalkboard style on 24 October 2026.
- Playful Turkish copy throughout, for example "Süre bitti, kalemler yere!" ("Time's up, pens down!").

## Hardest engineering problem

Dealing words without repeats. A word must never appear twice in one game, words nobody has seen in earlier games should come first, each level borrows a stable slice of the level below, and undo or summary edits must not put a seen word back in the deck.

- The fix: a per-game set of used words plus a per-level seen history in `UserDefaults`.
- Each new deck is built from words that are both unseen and unused in this game.
- When every word in a level has been seen, the history resets to just this game's words.
- If a list truly runs out mid-game, the game ends cleanly with a note instead of repeating.

A close second was keeping the word secret. The drawer holds the phone while teammates face the board, which led to the hold-to-reveal card with a short automatic peek.

## What I learned

- Curating beats volume. A quality pass flagged 127 words that were hard to draw or wrong for their level, and the lists shrank from 2,700 to 2,607. The "abstract words only in Dalí" rule made the levels feel distinct.
- Real-time timers should come from end dates, not from counting ticks.
- Party games need big targets, sound and haptic feedback, and forgiving corrections such as undo, summary edits and a short lock against double taps.
- Sounds need a natural decay: a cut-off tail reads as cheap, even when the sample itself is good.
- AI agents work best on mechanical work when it comes as written task files, with a separate build-and-review step afterwards.
